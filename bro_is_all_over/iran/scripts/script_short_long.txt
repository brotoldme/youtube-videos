Twenty-four hundred years ago, people in the Iranian desert were making ice. In summer. With no electricity.
On freezing winter nights, the clear desert sky pulls the heat out of the water. By morning, it is ice.
Workers packed it in straw and stored it in a mud-brick cellar. A chimney let warm air escape, so it stayed cold all summer.
That ice went into faloodeh, a frozen dessert of thin noodles, rose water, and lime.
The water came from the qanat: a tunnel that has carried water for twenty-five centuries.
